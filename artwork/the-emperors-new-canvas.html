The Emperor's New Canvas

Artist

Anita Moore

Medium

Empty frame, spotlight

Price

$3.1M

Provenance

The work consists of an empty frame and a spotlight. There is no canvas. There is no paint. There is no image. The frame is ornate. The spotlight is LED. The wall is white. The absence is the work. The work was acquired from the artist, who insisted on being paid in cash. The cash was placed inside the frame. The cash is not part of the work. The cash has been removed. The frame is empty. The work is empty. The price is not empty.

Artist Statement

Only those with true taste can see it. If you cannot see it, that says more about you than about the work. The work is there. The work has always been there. You just can't see it. This is your failure, not the work's. Hans Christian Andersen wrote about a king who wore nothing and everyone pretended to see the clothes. I have made the opposite: a frame that contains nothing and everyone pretends to see the art. The difference is that Andersen's story was a cautionary tale and mine is a business model. The frame is real. The spotlight is real. The wall is real. The art is invisible. The price is visible. $3.1M. If you can see the art, you'll know it's worth it. If you can't see the art, you're not the target audience. The target audience is people who can see invisible things. We have identified 14 such people. They are all on the board.

Curatorial Notes

Moore's work is a direct reference to Hans Christian Andersen's 'The Emperor's New Clothes,' with one critical inversion: in Andersen's tale, a child reveals the truth. In Moore's work, the truth is the price tag. The child would point at the empty frame and say 'there's nothing there.' Moore would say: 'Exactly. That's $3.1M, please.'

Guernica (Rebranded)

Artist

Pablow Picasso

Medium

Mixed media, focus-grouped

Price

$6.4M

Provenance

Originally a howl of anguish against the bombing of a Basque town. Tested poorly with key demographics (18-34, suburban, 'art-curious'). Revised per focus group feedback: 'too sad,' 'wished it were more uplifting,' 'would share on Instagram if it were happier.' All figures now smiling. Acquired by AIPCP in 2025 from a consultancy that specializes in 'brand optimization for historically significant cultural artifacts.'

Artist Statement

Picasso painted Guernica as a scream. The focus group said: 'the scream tested poorly.' So I made them smile. The horse smiles. The bull smiles. The mother holding her dead child smiles. The dead child smiles. The focus group said: 'much better, would share.' The bombing is the same. The suffering is the same. The only thing that changed is the expression. I think that's the point. Or maybe the point is that there is no point. The focus group didn't ask about the point. They asked about 'shareability.' It scored 8.2 out of 10. The original scored 2.1.

Curatorial Notes

The rebrand preserves Picasso's composition — every figure, every angle, every fragment — but replaces all expressions with a mild, focus-group-approved smile. The effect is not uplifting. The effect is horrifying. This is, perhaps, the most honest anti-war statement ever made: that suffering can be rebranded and nobody notices because everyone is smiling.

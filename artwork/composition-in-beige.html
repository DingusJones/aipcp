Composition in Beige

Artist

Gray Area

Medium

Beige on beige

Price

$890K

Provenance

The work is beige. It has always been beige. It will always be beige. It was acquired by AIPCP from the artist, who described it as 'the negation of color as resistance to chromatic hegemony.' The artist then went to get a coffee. The coffee was also beige. A latte.

Artist Statement

I chose beige because beige is the absence of choice. Red is a choice. Blue is a choice. Green is a choice. Beige is what happens when you stop choosing. Beige is the color of giving up. Beige is the color of offices. Beige is the color of waiting rooms. Beige is the color of the space between decisions. I painted beige on beige. The substrate is beige. The paint is beige. The frame is beige (the frame is not included; the frame would compromise the integrity of the beige). The wall is beige (the wall is not part of the work, but the wall is beige, so it doesn't matter). The viewer, standing in front of the work, wearing a beige sweater, is part of the work. The viewer doesn't know this. The viewer is beige. We are all beige. This is the work. The work is beige. The work is $890,000. The price is also beige. Everything is beige. I need to lie down.

Curatorial Notes

Gray Area's 400-word statement on 'the negation of color as resistance to chromatic hegemony' is notable for being 400 words about a beige painting. The painting is beige. The statement is not beige. The statement is vivid. The statement is the most colorful thing about the work. This irony appears to be unintentional.

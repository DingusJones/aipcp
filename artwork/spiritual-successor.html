Spiritual Successor

Artist

Copy Cat

Medium

Inspired by (inspired by inspired by)

Price

$2.2M

Provenance

This work is a 'spiritual successor' to another work in the AIPCP collection. That work was a 'spiritual successor' to a third work. That work was 'inspired by' a fourth work. That work was 'found at a yard sale in Connecticut.' The yard sale work was 'in the style of' a fifth work that has been lost. The lost work was 'attributed to' someone who may not have existed. The provenance chain is 6 links long. The original has not been seen since 1987. The yard sale was in 2003. Everything since then is 'inspired by.'

Artist Statement

I was inspired by a work that was inspired by a work that was inspired by a work that was found at a yard sale. I have not seen any of these works. I was told about them by someone who was told about them by someone who read about them in a catalog that has not been verified. The chain of inspiration is 6 links long. Each link adds nothing. Each link claims to add something. The 'something' is spiritual. The 'spiritual' is unverifiable. This is the medium. The medium is hearsay. I painted what I imagined the work looked like based on what someone told someone told someone it looked like. The result is a painting of a painting of a memory of a painting that may not exist. This is either profound or fraud. The price reflects the ambiguity.

Curatorial Notes

Copy Cat's work is a meditation on the telephone game of art history — each 'spiritual successor' moves further from whatever original may or may not have existed, until the chain of inspiration becomes the only tangible artifact. The yard sale in Connecticut has not been located. The yard sale may not have existed. The yard sale is the ur-text.

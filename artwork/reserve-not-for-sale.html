Reserve (Not for Sale)

Artist

Easel Cashley

Medium

Price tag, no artwork

Price

N/A

Provenance

The artwork is a price tag. The price tag reads '$1,400,000.' The artwork is not for sale. The not-for-sale-ness is the artwork. Acquiring the artwork means acquiring the concept of non-acquisition. The concept costs $1.4M. The price tag is not for sale. The concept is for sale. The concept cannot be sold because it is the concept of not being sold. This is the most expensive paradox in the collection.

Artist Statement

I made a price tag. I put a price on it. I said it's not for sale. The not-for-sale is the art. The price is the art. The tag is the art. If you buy it, it's not for sale, so you can't buy it, so you haven't bought it, so it's still not for sale, which is the art, which means the art still exists, which means you can buy it, which means it's for sale, which means the art is destroyed. I have created a piece of art that destroys itself when purchased. This is either the most conceptual art ever made or a tax avoidance strategy. Both. It's both.

Curatorial Notes

Cashley's work exists in a quantum state — it is both for sale and not for sale until observed by a buyer, at which point the act of purchase collapses the waveform and destroys the artwork's central premise. AIPCP has chosen not to resolve this paradox, as resolving it would lower the price.
